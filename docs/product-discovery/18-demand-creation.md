# 18 — Demand Creation

**Note on numbering:** requested as "16 — Demand Creation," renumbered to 18. See [00-overview.md](00-overview.md) for the full index.

For each of the 20 concepts (15), which noncustomer tier (11) it converts and what new demand it creates — the test of whether a concept is genuinely value-innovating versus just a feature: does it pull in people who weren't buying *anything* in this category before, not just people switching from a competitor.

| # | Concept | Noncustomer tier(s) converted | New demand created |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | MCP-native from day one | Tier 3 — AI-agent-driven/"vibe-coding" teams with no dedicated human tester | Demand for a test system an agent can operate directly, where none existed because no self-hosted tool offered agent access at all |
| 2 | BYO-LLM test generation | Tier 2 — regulated-DIY teams who refused SaaS AI tools on data-residency grounds | Demand for AI-assisted testing productivity *specifically among buyers who were previously forced to choose "no AI" as the price of self-hosting* |
| 3 | Human/AI accountability ledger | Tier 3 — platform/security teams who don't currently buy test tools at all | New demand from a buyer who was never in the category — evaluating this as an "AI agent governance" purchase, not a "QA tool" purchase |
| 4 | First-class Auditor role | Tier 3 — external auditors/compliance reviewers who've never been sold a test tool directly | Demand from a stakeholder who consumes test evidence today via manual handoff (spreadsheet, PDF) and has never been treated as a product user |
| 5 | Continuous/event-driven traceability | Tier 2 — regulated-DIY teams currently building RTMs by hand in Jira | Converts effort currently spent on manual reconstruction into demand for a tool, once the tool's advantage (continuous vs. reconstructed) is large enough to justify switching away from "free" DIY |
| 6 | Dual-standard document export | Tier 2 — teams that evaluated Squash TM/Jama, judged the cost/complexity not worth it, and do standards documentation by hand | Demand for standards-correct output without buying into a heavyweight ALM suite |
| 7 | Opt-in rigor / progressive adoption | Tier 2 — Agile teams who reject the category as "waterfall overhead" | New demand from teams who explicitly refuse category tooling today — only viable if the lightweight mode genuinely doesn't feel like the heavyweight tools they're rejecting |
| 8 | Usage-based self-hosted pricing | Tier 1 — QA teams inside large Jira orgs paying for seats unrelated to actual testers | Converts existing category spend into loyalty/expansion rather than creating new demand — a retention play more than a demand-creation play, included for completeness |
| 9 | Support-subscription revenue model | Tier 2 — regulated-DIY teams who won't buy Squash TM specifically because paywalled premium features look like a trust risk for compliance-critical functionality | Demand unlocked by removing the specific objection (paywalled compliance features) rather than by adding a new feature |
| 10 | Policy-as-code testing governance | Tier 3 — larger, process-mature engineering orgs who don't think of "testing policy enforcement" as a purchasable product today | Genuinely new demand — no competitor studied sells this as a distinct capability, so there's no existing spend to convert from |
| 11 | Community connector marketplace | Tier 1 — teams on Kiwi TCMS frustrated by the limited official plugin set (06) | Expands addressable automation-framework coverage beyond what any single vendor's engineering team could sell distinctly, converting "we can't integrate X" into new usage |
| 12 | Free ISTQB technique-practice sandbox | Tier 3 — ISTQB exam candidates and training providers, never previously a market for any competitor studied | Entirely new demand — a training/education use case with no current commercial supplier in this research |
| 13 | Auditor/consultant partner program | Tier 3 — compliance consultants and contract auditors who've never had a purpose-built multi-client tool | New demand from a channel partner who becomes a distribution multiplier, not just an end user — each consultant onboarding brings multiple client orgs |
| 14 | Evidence-bundle API for GRC/QMS platforms | Tier 3 — GRC/compliance teams who buy Vanta/Drata/Greenlight Guru and have never evaluated a test management tool | New demand from buyers who don't know this category exists as a solution to their problem — the clearest Salesforce-style third-tier move in this set |
| 15 | Production-incident-to-coverage linkage | Tier 1 — teams that treat testing as a checkbox and only reactively expand test coverage after incidents | Creates demand for expanded test coverage that didn't exist as a felt need until the tool makes the coverage gap visible and quantified |
| 16 | Community template/technique library | Tier 3 — solo developers/small teams who've never used a dedicated tool (04) | Lowers the adoption bar low enough to pull in "do nothing" teams who wouldn't otherwise justify evaluating a dedicated tool at all |
| 17 | Optional e-signature-grade approvals | Tier 2 — the most compliance-strict regulated buyers who currently use a separate e-signature tool alongside their test tool because neither one alone satisfies their audit bar | Converts a currently-split spend (test tool + separate e-signature tool) into demand for one consolidated purchase |
| 18 | "10-minute Docker Compose" commitment | Tier 1 — teams who evaluated Squash TM, found it too heavy, and gave up | Recovers demand that already existed but was lost to adoption friction, not created from nothing — a conversion play more than a demand-creation play |
| 19 | Agent-governance framing for security/platform buyers | Tier 3 — security/platform teams with budget for AI-agent governance tooling, who don't currently consider test management software relevant to that budget | New demand from an entirely different budget line than QA — the single clearest new-demand play in this set given how fast MCP/agent governance is becoming its own buying category (06) |
| 20 | "Coexist with Jira" positioning | Tier 1 — Jira-centric teams currently paying for Xray/Zephyr who are cost-sensitive but don't want to leave Jira | Converts existing category spend (switching cost, not new demand) — included because it's the fastest-to-realize revenue even though it's not value-innovation in the strict Blue Ocean sense |

## Insight

**INSIGHT:** Concepts #3, #4, #10, #12, #13, #14, and #19 are the genuine third-tier, new-demand plays — they pull in buyers who were never evaluating this category at all (compliance consultants, GRC teams, security/platform teams, ISTQB trainees, auditors). Concepts #8, #18, and #20 are conversion/retention plays dressed as demand creation — real revenue opportunities, but they compete for existing category spend rather than expanding the market, and should be evaluated by different criteria (win rate against named competitors, not total addressable non-market).

**RECOMMENDATION:** If forced to pick a small MVP-relevant subset (not decided here — belongs in the not-yet-produced MVP-scoping doc), concepts #1–#4 and #14 form the most internally consistent bet: they all derive from the same underlying `Actor`/traceability data model (07), require no new business-model invention, and directly target the two customer segments (regulated mid-market + GRC-adjacent unexplored noncustomers) that 11 and 13 independently identified as the highest-confidence whitespace. Concepts #10, #12, #13, and #19 are real options but each implies a materially different go-to-market motion (enterprise governance sales, education/training funnel, channel-partner program, security-buyer positioning respectively) — bundling all of them into one MVP would dilute focus across four different buyers before any one of them is validated.
