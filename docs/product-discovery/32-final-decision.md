# 32 — Final Decision

**Note on numbering:** requested as "30 — Final Decision," renumbered to 32. See [00-overview.md](00-overview.md) for the full index.

## Recommendation: **GO — narrowly, bounded to the 26 MVP + 27 experiment only.**

This is not a recommendation to build the company. It is a recommendation to spend the next 30–45 days running the already-scoped, already-cheap validation cycle (26, 27), with the explicit understanding that the decision after that experiment is genuinely open — full commitment, pivot to #14, or kill — and pre-committed to being decided by the data, not by momentum. Per Decision-Making principle 16, an MVP should not be built because the idea is interesting; this recommendation isn't based on the idea being interesting, it's based on five specific, evidenced reasons below, several of which (2, 4) are exactly what makes a *bounded* test the right size of commitment rather than a full build-out.

## The five strongest reasons for this decision

**1. Market timing is genuinely strong and dated, not evergreen rationale (31).** The EU AI Act's high-risk AI obligations became enforceable August 2, 2026, with data-governance requirements that explicitly cannot be outsourced to an LLM vendor — a real, checkable, recent regulatory event, not a speculative trend. Open-weight LLM quality closed most of the gap to frontier models on coding tasks in 2026 specifically (Qwen 3.6 Plus scoring competitively with Claude Opus/GPT-5.4 on SWE-Bench Verified) — a technical precondition for this concept that didn't reliably exist 12–18 months earlier. Both facts argue for testing now rather than waiting, because the window this timing creates is unlikely to stay open indefinitely.

**2. The structural whitespace is the best-triangulated finding in this entire discovery process.** Four independent analyses — the AI/MCP competitive landscape (06), the strategy canvas (09), the alternative-industries analysis (12), and the strategic-groups gap analysis (13) — arrived at the same conclusion (no competitor combines self-hosting with AI/MCP) without being designed to converge. That's a materially stronger evidentiary basis than any single competitive analysis alone, and it's the reason this idea survived 21's stricter competition test at all.

**3. The moat is weak, and that specifically caps how large a bet this deserves right now, not whether to bet at all (28, 29, 30).** No durable data, technology, brand, network-effect, or distribution moat exists today; the one real advantage (BYO-LLM's structural cost benefit) is non-exclusive. A well-resourced competitor could plausibly close this gap within 6–12 months (28). This is a strong argument against a large, fast, all-in commitment — and an equally strong argument *for* a fast, cheap test, because if the position is this replicable, the cost of finding out slowly is higher than the cost of finding out fast.

**4. The proposed next step is genuinely low-cost and time-boxed with pre-committed, objective kill triggers already written (26, 27).** This isn't a request to build the full 07 ERD or the full traceability/RBAC depth — it's a single-hypothesis MVP with defined success thresholds (activation ≥60%, sustained adoption ≥50%, paid-LOI ≥20%, quality-usable ≥60%) set *before* data collection specifically to prevent post-hoc rationalization. The downside of running this test is small and bounded; the downside of skipping it and building further on unvalidated willingness-to-pay is not.

**5. Willingness-to-pay evidence for the chosen concept is real but the weakest primary evidence in this discovery set — exactly the kind of gap a cheap direct test closes fast.** 22 ranked all six competition-test survivors by payment evidence and placed the chosen concept (#1+#2) third/fourth of six, behind #14 and #19 — both of which were explicitly rejected as the wedge (23) on feasibility and "why us" grounds, not because their money evidence was weaker. That's a real, acknowledged trade-off, not an oversight — and it's precisely the open question 27's experiment is built to answer with primary data instead of more secondary research, which this discovery process has now exhausted the useful yield of.

## Evidence still missing — what this discovery process could not answer

- **Bottom-up TAM** for the self-hosted + regulated/security-conscious + AI-adopting intersection specifically. 01 flagged this gap at the very start of this process and it was never closed — no source in the entire discovery set sizes this sub-segment.
- **Direct willingness-to-pay signal from real target customers**, not adjacent-category pricing benchmarks (Qase, Vanta, HashiCorp). Everything in 22 is evidence the *category* gets paid for, not evidence *this specific buyer* pays *this specific product* — the exact gap 27's experiment exists to close.
- **Local/BYO-LLM test-case-generation quality specifically.** The coding-benchmark evidence found in 31 (SWE-Bench Verified) is a reasonable proxy but not a direct measurement of test-case-generation quality, which has its own failure modes (test relevance, coverage judgment, technique appropriateness) that general coding benchmarks don't capture.
- **Whether the deferred MVP scope is what actually gates production conversion.** 29's cause 10 flagged this directly: pilots may love the AI-generation capability and still not convert to production use if the real switching decision depends on traceability/RBAC/migration tooling that was deliberately cut from the MVP. 27's experiment should be extended with the qualitative question 29 recommended — "what would you need before actually replacing your current tool" — before this is fully answered.
- **GRC/QMS partner willingness** to integrate an evidence feed (the open dependency behind #14, flagged in 20/22) — not required to run 27's experiment, but should be pursued in parallel via direct outreach, since #14 is the named pivot candidate if the current concept's experiment underperforms.

## Decision conditions after the 27 experiment runs

- **If 27's thresholds are met:** proceed to a real MVP build-out (beyond the concierge-hybrid version) and begin closing the two remaining evidence gaps (bottom-up TAM, GRC partner willingness) in parallel — this becomes a genuine GO on building the company, not just the test.
- **If 27's thresholds are not met, but the failure-mode diagnostics (27) point to setup friction or model quality rather than a rejected value proposition:** iterate once on the specific failure mode, don't kill the concept outright — a low-activation, high-quality result is a different problem than a high-activation, low-adoption one, and 27 already specifies how to tell them apart.
- **If 27's thresholds are not met and the value proposition itself reads as rejected** (low activation *and* low paid-LOI *and* pilots say they'd still rather use ChatGPT/Cursor manually): **pivot to #14 (evidence-bundle API)**, contingent on the GRC-partner-willingness conversations having been run in parallel — it had the strongest standalone willingness-to-pay evidence of the six competition-test survivors (22) and simply requires the external dependency to be resolved first, which parallel outreach should have already started answering.

## A serious alternative worth naming explicitly, not burying

Given 30's weak-moat finding, one option this discovery process did not fully explore but should be named directly: **rather than compete against Kiwi TCMS, build the MCP server and BYO-LLM generation capability as a plugin/contribution to Kiwi TCMS itself** (or Squash TM). This trades owning a standalone product for an instant, already-large installed base and community — a real answer to 28/29's distribution and moat concerns, at the cost of not capturing standalone company value. This is precisely the kind of "substantially different from the original idea" recommendation this discovery process's own mandate calls for being willing to make — it is not evaluated further here because it changes the business model question (25) entirely, but it deserves a real conversation before committing to the standalone-product path, not a footnote.
